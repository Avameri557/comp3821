\section{Social Optimum: Diamond Network}

\subsection{Network}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includestandalone[width=0.65\textwidth]{../diagrams/diamond}
  \caption{Diamond network. $x$ is the number of agents on an edge in a timestep.}
\end{figure}

All agents travel $1 \to 6$ on one of four disjoint routes $R_k = 1 \to k \to 6$. Adding its two edges,
$R_k$ costs $A_k + B_k n$ per agent with $n$ agents on it:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
& $R_2$ & $R_3$ & $R_4$ & $R_5$ \\
\midrule
$A_k$ & 2.0   & 2.2   & 2.4   & 2.6   \\
$B_k$ & 0.026 & 0.024 & 0.021 & 0.018 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Cheaper routes congest faster. Every route's edges are crossed at steps 0 and 1, so timing plays no role.

\subsection{Derivation}

With $n_k$ agents on $R_k$,
\[
  SC = \sum_k \bigl(A_k n_k + B_k n_k^2\bigr), \qquad \sum_k n_k = N.
\]
At the optimum every used route has the same marginal cost $\lambda$:
\[
  A_k + 2B_k n_k = \lambda \quad\Longrightarrow\quad n_k = \frac{\lambda - A_k}{2B_k},
\]
with $\lambda$ chosen so that $\sum_k n_k = N$. A route is used only if $A_k < \lambda$, so routes open
cheapest first as $N$ grows; $R_5$ needs $N > 24.6$.

\paragraph{Whole agents.} The $j$-th agent on $R_k$ adds $A_k + B_k(2j - 1)$, which grows with $j$. So
adding agents one at a time to the cheapest route is exactly optimal.

\subsection{Results}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
$N$ & $n_2$ & $n_3$ & $n_4$ & $n_5$ & $\lambda$ & $\SCopt$ & $\SCopt/N$ \\
\midrule
20 & 10 & 7  & 3  & 0  & 2.53 & 46.565  & 2.32825 \\
50 & 17 & 14 & 11 & 8  & 2.88 & 127.911 & 2.55822 \\
80 & 23 & 21 & 19 & 17 & 3.20 & 219.121 & 2.73901 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Optimal number of agents on each route.}
\end{table}

\begin{itemize}
  \item $N = 20$: $\lambda \approx 2.53 < 2.6$, so $R_5$ stays empty.
  \item With fractional agents, each agent pays $(A_k + \lambda)/2$, so each cheaper route's users pay
        exactly 0.1 less: the optimum deliberately loads cheap routes more.
  \item $\SCopt/N$ rises from 2.33 to 2.74 as $N$ grows from 20 to 80.
\end{itemize}
